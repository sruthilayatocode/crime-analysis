CrimeSense: A Unified Crime Hotspot Visualization, Zone Classification and Personal Safety Platform with Pattern Recurrence Analysis for Vellore, India

Abstract— We developed CrimeSense to help users explore crime reports in Vellore and examine reported incidents around a selected location. The current system uses an SQLite database containing 596 news-derived incident records and includes an administrative dashboard for viewing and managing these records. We present an interpretable zone classification model that marks locations green, yellow, or red using spatial and temporal decay. To support pedestrian safety, we formulate design specifications for a pre-visit destination advisory, an OpenStreetMap-based safe haven finder relying on natural surveillance, and an automated red-zone inactivity state machine. In addition, we describe a pattern recurrence formulation applying Knox space-time permutation tests and association rules to identify sequential crime trends. Finally, we report database verification audits, role-separated authentication checks, and privacy safeguards conforming to the Indian Digital Personal Data Protection Act, followed by a formal evaluation protocol.
Keywords—Crime hotspot visualization, spatio-temporal risk classification, safe haven discovery, near-repeat pattern recurrence, personal safety, emergency SOS alerting, news-derived crime analytics, spatial decision support.

I. Introduction

Crime incidents do not distribute uniformly across urban terrain. Across decades of empirical spatial criminology, research has confirmed the "law of crime concentration" [1], which posits that a disproportionately large fraction of criminal infractions concentrates within a remarkably narrow collection of micro-places, intersections, and street segments [2]. Criminological frameworks explain this phenomenon through Routine Activity Theory [3], wherein crime requires the convergence in space and time of motivated perpetrators, suitable targets, and the absence of capable guardianship. Crime Pattern Theory [4] further establishes that perpetrators typically operate within overlapping awareness spaces formed by everyday activity nodes and transition pathways.

Modern law enforcement agencies routinely leverage spatial analytics, such as Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) [5], [6] and density-based spatial clustering (DBSCAN) [7], to target patrol strategies, achieving notable localized crime reductions [8]. However, existing systems exhibit two fundamental paradigm gaps that leave ordinary citizens vulnerable: (1) Analytical Asymmetry: advanced hotspot mapping is designed strictly for institutional analysts, while official statistics [11] are published annually at municipal scale; and (2) Purely Reactive Safety Paradigms: commercial mobile applications [12] function as passive panic buttons, triggering only after an attack occurs, with zero environmental intelligence.

To bridge these divides, this paper introduces CrimeSense for Vellore, Tamil Nadu, India. We mine an authenticated news-corpus of 596 verified reports [13], [14] and build an integrated dual-interface platform connecting administrative hotspot analytics with civilian protection.

II. Related Work

Spatial crime analysis relies on clustering algorithms to translate discrete event points into continuous risk surfaces. Classical Kernel Density Estimation (KDE) calculates spatial density surfaces [19], while temporal extensions weight recent incidents higher [6]. DBSCAN identifies clusters of arbitrary geometry without demanding predefined grid boundaries, isolating isolated crimes as noise [7], [20]. Predictive architectures using self-exciting point processes [10] or Risk Terrain Modeling [9], [21] demand massive dispatch registries rarely open in developing nations [22], [23].

The near-repeat hypothesis posits that once an offense occurs, neighboring targets face temporary surges in victimization risk [17], [24], evaluated via Knox permutation tests [16]. In urban navigation, safe routing trades distance against risk [25]. Jane Jacobs [15] observed that vibrant streetscapes with commercial establishments foster safety through "eyes on the street", corroborated by Oscar Newman's Defensible Space theory [26]. III. System Architecture & Design

CrimeSense is structured as a decoupled multi-tier architecture uniting a React mobile resident app, desktop admin console, Flask REST API with role enforcement and Google OAuth 2.0 verification [28], and single-source SQLite persistence.

A. Dynamic Zone Classification Engine

For query point q, candidate crimes within radius ρ (default 1000 m) are filtered via great-circle Haversine distance [29]:

d(q, i) = 2R · arcsin(√a),   R = 6371 km   (1)

Each incident i contributes based on severity si ∈ [2, 10], linear distance decay δ(d) = 1 - (d / ρ), and recency weight w(a) ∈ {1.0, 0.7, 0.4}:

R(q) = ∑i ∈ N(q) si · δ(d(q, i)) · w(ai)   (2)
Z(q) = min(100, 100 · [R(q) / Rref])   (3)

Scores map to Green (Z < 30), Yellow (30 ≤ Z < 60), and Red (Z ≥ 60).

B. Safe-Haven Spatial Optimization

When an individual feels unsafe, CrimeSense identifies nearby public social hubs (supermarkets, 24/7 pharmacies, police posts, clinics) where natural surveillance operates. Candidate havens within 2000 m are ranked by utility score:

H(p) = wc · o(p, t) · exp(-dp / λ) · (1 - Zp / 100)   (4)

where wc is guardianship weight (Police: 1.0, Hospital: 0.9, Market: 0.7, Pharmacy: 0.65), o(p, t) is opening status, and Zp penalizes havens in hotspots.

C. Pre-Visit Destination Advisory

When a resident checks a prospective destination, a rule engine produces an advisory card detailing incident counts, date ranges, crime types, and guidance (e.g., "Elevated severe reports; companion travel recommended; prefer daylight") without stigmatizing neighborhoods.

D. Autonomous Red-Zone Dwell Escalation

Manual SOS triggers via a 2-second press with 5-second countdown. For silent incapacitation, a finite-state machine monitors location heartbeats in Red Zones: if stationary for 10 minutes without screen interaction, an "Are you safe?" challenge is presented for 60 seconds. Lack of response triggers automated emergency SMS dispatch with last-known coordinates.

E. Pattern-Recurrence Analytics

Knox space-time interaction permutation tests compute:

X = ∑i < j 1[dij ≤ Δs] · 1[|ti - tj| ≤ Δt]   (5)

over Δs ∈ {250, 500, 1000} m and Δt ∈ {7, 14, 30} days across 999 permutations, coupled with sequential association rule lift (Lift > 1.2) to detect repeating crime patterns.

V. Proposed Evaluation Protocol

We specify rolling temporal split evaluation using Predictive Accuracy Index (PAI = [n/N] / [a/A] ≥ 2.5), Knox Near-Repeat Ratio (NRR ≥ 1.35, p < 0.01), Valid@5 haven accuracy (≥ 85%), and System Usability Scale (SUS ≥ 78.0).

VI. Discussion & Ethics

CrimeSense actively avoids pejorative labeling by classifying zones as "Reported Incidents," excludes enforcement prediction loops, and adheres to India's DPDP Act 2023 through opt-in tracking and audited admin logs [35].

VII. Conclusion

CrimeSense bridges the divide between institutional spatial intelligence and proactive citizen protection. Future work includes field haven audits and rolling temporal calibration.
